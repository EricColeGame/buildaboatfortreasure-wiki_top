## Practical Boat-Building Reasoning

The game does not publish an official engineering manual, so everything below is **player reasoning**, not documented statistics. Still, these principles hold up across most builder-type physics games and are a reasonable starting point.

**Buoyancy comes first.** A boat floats because it displaces enough water relative to its weight. If you stack heavy materials high above the waterline, you raise the center of gravity and increase the chance of tipping. Keep your heaviest structural blocks low and centered.

**Thrust is a tradeoff.** Adding more propulsion can help, but it also adds weight, which affects buoyancy. If your boat sinks when you add engines, the problem is usually mass distribution, not engine count.

**Protection matters more than speed.** Obstacles in this genre tend to damage whatever they touch. A slightly slower boat with a reinforced front and a protected core often outperforms a fast, fragile build. Think about which parts must survive and which are acceptable losses.

**Test early, test often.** The fastest way to learn what works is to launch frequently and observe failure points. Where does your boat break? Where does it flip? That feedback loop is more useful than any guide.

## Reading the Game as an Adventure

Because the genre is Adventure, the loop is not purely about raw construction. You are navigating stages, and stages introduce hazards. The exact number of stages, the specific obstacles, and the rewards per stage are **not documented in the official description**, and this guide will not invent them.

What you can do is treat each stage as a diagnostic. If you fail repeatedly at the same point, the issue is probably structural rather than random. If you fail at random points, your build may be too fragile overall.

For players who want more durability, the advertised Mega Member and Royal Member health bonuses are the clearest documented lever. Whether that bonus changes your strategy depends on how you build.

## Community Observations vs. Official Documentation
