## The Core Build Loop, in General Terms

The game gives you a build plot, a palette of blocks, and a launch button. Everything after that is physics. Because the developer does not publish stat tables, the following is player reasoning rather than documented data — useful as a mental model, not as gospel.

A boat that reaches the treasure has to solve four problems at once:

- **Buoyancy.** Your craft must displace enough water to support its own mass.
- **Weight distribution.** Heavy parts placed high make you top-heavy and easy to flip.
- **Thrust.** Something has to push you forward, and the push has to survive contact with obstacles.
- **Protection.** The front of the boat takes the hits, so that is where reinforcement earns its keep.

Every build choice trades one of these against another. More armor means more mass. More thrust often means more exposed parts.

## Designing for Buoyancy and Stability

Beginners usually overbuild. The instinct is to add blocks until the boat looks impressive, then watch it sink at the starting line. A steadier approach is to build the smallest hull that floats with your intended payload, then expand only where you need to.

Keep your center of mass low. Wide, flat bottoms resist tipping better than narrow, tall ones. If a design keeps capsizing at a particular obstacle, the fix is often to move weight downward rather than to add more floatation on top.

This is general design reasoning drawn from how physics-driven builders typically behave. It is not a claim about specific block values, because the official sources do not publish those.

## Making It Move and Survive the Obstacles

Propulsion and protection compete for the same real estate. A useful habit is to treat the front of the boat as a sacrificial zone. If the leading edge is going to absorb impacts, it makes sense to reinforce or shape it so a single hit does not separate your thrust from your hull.

Player guides on platforms like YouTube and the Roblox community forums often show stage-specific solutions. Those are third-party observations, not developer documentation, and they go stale as the game is updated. The last official update was October 9, 2026, so any guide older than that may describe a version of the game that no longer exists.

If you want a repeatable process, use this:

1. Build a minimal floating hull and launch it with no extras.
2. Add propulsion and confirm you can move in a straight line.
3. Add protection only at the points that actually take damage.
4. Re-launch after each change so you know what caused a failure.

That loop costs you runs but saves you from guessing.
