## How to Reason About Boat Design

Everything in this section is player reasoning and general design logic, not documented statistics. Treat it as a framework for thinking, not a set of guaranteed rules.

A boat in this game has to satisfy competing demands. It needs to float, it needs to move, and it needs to survive contact with obstacles. Those three goals pull against each other.

**Buoyancy and weight.** More blocks generally mean more mass. A wide, spread-out base tends to be more stable than a tall, narrow stack, because the center of mass stays low relative to the water line. If your build tips over immediately, the problem is usually distribution rather than total weight.

**Thrust and control.** Propulsion gets you moving, but too much power on a light frame makes a boat skittish. If you find yourself overshooting or flipping on acceleration, adding structure near the base often helps more than adding more thrust.

**Protection.** Obstacles are the reason a boat needs armor. Placing durable material at the front and around whatever you cannot afford to lose is a common approach. The trade-off is always the same: protection adds weight, and weight affects buoyancy and speed.

**Iteration.** The most useful habit is to change one thing at a time. If you swap three variables and the run improves, you have learned nothing about which change mattered.

| Design goal | Common player approach | Trade-off to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Stability | Wider base, lower center of mass | More blocks, more mass |
| Speed | Stronger or additional propulsion | Harder to control when light |
| Survival | Armor at the front and around critical parts | Added weight reduces buoyancy |
