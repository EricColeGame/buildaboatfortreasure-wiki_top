## Boat Design Secrets That Veterans Swear By

### Every boat has three jobs

A working boat has to float, move, and survive. Most failed builds try to do all three with the same blocks. Separating those jobs is the first real secret of boat building.

| Component | Its job | Community notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hull blocks | Float and hold everything together | Wide, low footprints tip over less than tall towers |
| Wheels | Handle ramps and land sections | Almost mandatory once stages mix terrain |
| Thrusters or jets | Forward speed and burst power | Best saved for gaps, ramps, and quick escapes |
| Balloons | Lift you over hazards | Powerful but can flip a boat if unbalanced |
| Sails | Cheap, steady forward motion | Great early game, weaker when wind matters |
| Glue or weld blocks | Keep parts attached | Skipping this is the #1 cause of mid-run breakups |
| Anchors | Hold position | Handy when waiting on co-op teammates |

### Weight and balance matter more than looks

Player experience suggests that heavy boats sink faster, turn slower, and struggle on ramps. If your boat keeps nose-diving, remove blocks before you add power. Move heavy components toward the center and keep the front narrow so it slips through gaps instead of catching on them.

### Build redundancy, not perfection

The best builders expect parts to break. A backup thruster, a spare balloon cluster, or a detachable front section means one bad hit doesn't end your run. Think of your boat as three independent systems — float, move, survive — so losing one doesn't kill the other two.

## Stage Survival Secrets: Beating Hazards Without Rebuilding

Most stage failures come down to a small number of recurring hazards. Preparing for them in the build menu is far cheaper than improvising on the water.

| Hazard type | Common mistake | Smarter counter |
|---|---|---|
| Waterfalls and drops | Heavy, top-loaded boat | Balloons or a light hull to survive the fall |
| Spikes and grinders | Full speed ahead | Controlled, slow approach with a narrow front |
| Projectiles and cannons | Tall structure blocking your view | Low profile with the core protected behind blocks |
| Fire and lava zones | All-wood build | Fire-resistant materials where your inventory allows |
| Narrow gaps | Wide, sprawling boat | Modular narrow nose section you can sacrifice |
| Long flat stretches | Running out of thrust | Sails or efficient propulsion for cruising |

**Player experience tip:** build your control seat near the center of the boat, not the back. You'll see hazards sooner and your boat will handle better with weight distributed around you.

## A Practical Routine for New Players

Here's a simple loop that works whether you're brand new or returning after a break:

1. **Scout one new stage** with a cheap, disposable boat just to see the hazards.
2. **Rebuild specifically for those hazards** rather than copying your last design.

5. **Try the new stage again** with the improved boat and a backup plan.
