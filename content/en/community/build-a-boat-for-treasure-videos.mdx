## Build Reasoning: Buoyancy, Weight, Thrust, Protection

Everything in this section is **player reasoning**, not developer documentation. The official sources do not publish block stats, mass values, or thrust figures. Treat these as design principles you can test, not rules the game enforces.

**Buoyancy first.** A boat that sinks never reaches the treasure. Before adding anything decorative or offensive, confirm the hull floats on its own. Build small, launch, observe. A design that floats at half size usually floats at full size; a design that barely floats at half size usually does not.

**Weight is cumulative and unforgiving.** Every block you add is more mass to carry. This is why "just add more armor" is a trap — armor that sinks you is worse than no armor. Add structure in layers and re-test after each layer rather than building the whole thing and hoping.

**Thrust should match mass.** If your craft is slow and wallowing, the problem is usually the ratio between how much it weighs and how much forward force you are applying. Adding more thrust without addressing weight often just burns through your build budget.

**Protection is about placement, not volume.** Obstacles tend to hit predictable zones — the front, the underside, the top. Reinforcing those areas costs less than armoring the entire hull and leaves more room for buoyancy and thrust.

**Keep a rebuild habit.** A design you can repair quickly beats a perfect design you cannot reproduce. If a run fails, you want to know exactly which change caused it.

## How to Approach a Run Without Wasting Attempts

Since the official page does not document stage counts or obstacle layouts, any specific "stage 12 has spikes" claim you read elsewhere is third-party observation, not developer fact. That does not make it useless — it just means you should verify it in your own run before building around it.

A practical loop looks like this:

1. Build a minimal, floatable test craft.
2. Launch it and identify the **first** thing that kills you.
3. Change exactly one variable — mass, thrust, or protection — to address that cause.
4. Repeat until the failure point moves further out.
5. Only then start investing in gold-earning efficiency.

The reason to isolate variables is that boats fail for compounding reasons. If you add armor, thrust, and weight at once, you learn nothing. If you add only armor and the run improves, you have learned something real.
