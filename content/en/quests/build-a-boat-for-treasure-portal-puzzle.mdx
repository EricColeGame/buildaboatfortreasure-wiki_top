## Designing a boat that survives: general reasoning, not documented stats

Because no official block specifications are published, the following is player reasoning about design tradeoffs rather than confirmed numbers. Treat it as a thinking framework, not a build recipe.

Three forces fight each other on every plot: buoyancy, weight, and thrust.

| Design concern | What it usually means in practice | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Buoyancy | Enough low-density volume to keep the hull above water | Too little float volume, boat sinks before the first obstacle |
| Weight | Every block adds mass; heavy armor slows you down | Over-armoring the hull until thrust cannot move it |
| Thrust | Engines or propulsion must overcome total weight and drag | Too few propulsion blocks for the mass carried |
| Protection | Shielding the front and flanks absorbs impacts | Armor packed so tightly it adds mass without coverage |

A practical approach many players describe is to build the smallest viable hull first, confirm it floats and moves, then add protection incrementally and test after each change. If a run fails, the question is almost always "did I add mass without adding thrust, or thrust without buoyancy?" Changing one variable at a time makes that diagnosable.

Two more general points worth internalizing:

- Shape matters as much as volume. A wide, flat footprint resists tipping on uneven water more than a tall narrow stack.
- Redundancy helps. If a single critical block is the only thing keeping you afloat or moving, losing it ends the run. Spreading function across multiple blocks increases survival odds.

These are design heuristics, not documented mechanics. The game does not publish a block compendium, so no article can give you exact placement rules.
