## Building a Boat That Survives: Design Reasoning

What follows is general design reasoning that experienced players tend to converge on. It is reasoning, not documented stats — the official page publishes no build formulas, and the physics are best learned by testing on the build plot.

**Buoyancy and weight.** A wide, flat base spreads weight and resists tipping. Piling heavy material high above the waterline raises your center of mass and makes rolls more likely. Think of the hull as the foundation and everything else as cargo.

**Thrust and steering.** Propulsion placed low and centered tends to push predictably. Off-center thrust creates a turning bias you then have to correct constantly. Steering that responds before an obstacle arrives is worth more than raw speed, because stages are obstacle courses rather than races.

**Protection.** A shielded front section absorbs the first hit from most obstacles. Critical components — whatever keeps you moving and whatever keeps you afloat — benefit from being enclosed rather than exposed at the edges.

**Iteration.** Build, launch, watch what breaks first, and rebuild that section. This loop teaches more than any written list, and it is the reason specific blueprint advice ages poorly while design principles do not.
