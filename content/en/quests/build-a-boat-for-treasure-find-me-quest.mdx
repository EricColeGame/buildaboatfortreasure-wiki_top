## Why Hidden Quests Change How You Play

Most players treat Build a Boat For Treasure as a straight line: build something that floats, launch it, survive the stages, grab the prize. The Build a Boat for Treasure find me quest breaks that rhythm. It asks you to stop, look around, and solve a small mystery that has nothing to do with speed or hull strength.

That is exactly why it matters. The main loop rewards brute force and good engineering. Quest content rewards attention. Players who learn to read prompts and scan their surroundings end up understanding every stage better, and they usually walk away with extra gold, badges, and cosmetic parts that pure speedrunners never collect.

Treat the Build a Boat for Treasure find me quest as a treasure hunt layered inside a treasure hunt. Instead of steering straight for the glowing objective, you slow down, read what the game is telling you, and search the map for whatever that hint is pointing at. The prize is rarely enormous, but the bragging rights are real, because most people sail right past it.

## What the Build a Boat for Treasure Find Me Quest Actually Is

In Build a Boat For Treasure, everything orbits one loop: assemble a boat from blocks, launch it, and survive the journey to the treasure. Quests sit outside that loop. They are optional objectives that ask you to do something the main path never requires.

The "find me" style quest is a search objective. A prompt, sign, or hint appears somewhere in the world and asks you to locate something specific: an object, a character, or a marker hidden in the scenery. Nothing on your screen points toward it. There is no arrow, no distance counter, no glowing outline. That is the entire design, and it is also why so many players complete a stage without ever realizing a quest was available.

Community reports describe the wording, location, and trigger conditions differently, and that makes sense. The game has been updated repeatedly since launch, and quest content gets added, moved, or retired over time. Treat any single set of directions you read online as a starting point rather than a guarantee, then verify it yourself in a live server.

### Why players chase it

- **Rewards.** Quest objectives typically pay out in gold, badges, or cosmetic blocks you cannot buy.
- **Map knowledge.** Hunting for a hidden target forces you to explore areas you normally skip.
- **Build skills.** A boat that can turn tightly and hover in place is genuinely useful for searching.
- **Completionism.** Badge collectors care about quests long after they stop caring about distance records.

## Step-by-Step: Running the Find Me Quest

There is no universal script, because the target changes. What does carry over is a repeatable method. Work through these steps in order and you will find almost any search objective the game throws at you.

| Step | What to do | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the prompt twice before moving | Quest text often contains the only hint you get |
| 2 | Stop your boat and scan 360 degrees | Targets are frequently behind the spawn direction |
| 3 | Check above and below your deck level | Hidden objects sit on ledges, under platforms, and in water |
| 4 | Note landmarks, then return with a smaller boat | A nimble build reaches gaps a big hull cannot |
| 5 | Ask in chat or check a public server | Other players often confirm a target's general area |
| 6 | Write down what you find | Notes save you from re-searching the same stage twice |

### Working the search properly

Start with a slow lap. Sail the perimeter of the area first, then spiral inward. Most hidden targets sit near the edges of a zone rather than dead center, because the middle of the map is where the main path runs.

If you spot something that looks out of place, a floating prop, an oddly placed block, a shape that does not match the surrounding terrain, investigate it before you move on. Player experience consistently points to "odd detail" as the single best indicator that you are close.

Finally, do not trust your memory. Stages blur together after a few runs. A quick note about which stage, which side of the map, and what the target looked like will save you real time later.

## Boat Builds That Make Quest Hunting Easier

Your everyday racing boat is usually the wrong tool for this job. Quest hunting rewards control and visibility over raw top speed.

| Build trait | Why it helps | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Short, compact hull | Fits into narrow gaps and under overhangs | Lower top speed |
| Multiple thrusters, angled | Lets you hover and reverse on demand | Higher part cost |
| Low, flat deck | Keeps your camera clear of the boat | Less room for cargo |
| Minimal decoration | Nothing blocks your view of the horizon | Less impressive to other players |
| Wide, stable base | Prevents tipping when you stop suddenly | Slightly heavier |
| Open seating area | Easy to hop out and look around | No protection from hazards |

A practical approach is to keep two builds: a fast boat for normal progression and a small, thruster-heavy scout for quest runs. Switching between them takes a minute and makes searching dramatically less frustrating. If you would rather not maintain two designs, add a couple of angled thrusters to your main boat so you can pivot in place.

Keep at least one light source and a way to reach higher ground on your scout. Many hidden objectives sit just out of reach of a standard hull, and a simple ramp or platform attachment solves that problem cheaply.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to complete the Build a Boat for Treasure find me quest to progress?**
No. Quests are optional side content layered on top of the main build-and-sail loop. You can reach the treasure and advance through stages without ever touching a quest objective. The rewards are the reason to bother, not progression gating.

**Can I do quests in a private server or with friends?**
Player experience suggests that most quest objectives work the same regardless of who else is in the server, though some objectives are explicitly social and expect other players to be present. If a target seems impossible to trigger, try a public server before assuming you are looking in the wrong place.

**Why does the prompt never appear for me?**
The most common causes are timing and version differences. Some objectives only activate under specific conditions, and content is occasionally added, moved, or retired between updates. Sail the stage slowly, check chat, and compare notes with other players before you conclude the quest is gone.

**Are the rewards worth the time?**
For gold alone, probably not compared with efficient stage runs. For badges, exclusive cosmetic parts, and the map knowledge you pick up along the way, absolutely. The Build a Boat for Treasure find me quest is best treated as a change of pace rather than a farming route.
