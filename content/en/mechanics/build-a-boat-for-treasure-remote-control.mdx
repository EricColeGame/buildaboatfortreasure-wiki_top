## What the Game Actually Is

Build A Boat For Treasure is a building-and-sailing experience. You construct a vessel out of blocks on a build plot, then send it through obstacle-filled stages to earn gold. The core loop is simple to describe and surprisingly deep to play: design, launch, survive, earn, redesign.

That loop is also why the game has held an audience since 2016. Your boat is not a fixed character class; it is something you author, and every failure teaches you something about your own design choices.

## The Four Forces Behind Every Design

Third-party guides and experienced players tend to frame boat building around the same handful of concerns. Treat the following as community reasoning and general design logic, not documented statistics:

- **Buoyancy.** Your boat has to float. Blocks that add lift keep you above the surface; too much dense structure and you sink before the first obstacle.
- **Weight distribution.** A top-heavy boat rolls. Keeping mass low and centered makes a vessel far more stable in motion.
- **Thrust.** Something has to move you forward. More or bigger propulsion is not automatically better, because it usually costs weight and space you could spend elsewhere.
- **Protection.** Obstacles damage your boat. Shielding the parts you cannot afford to lose matters more than armoring everything equally.

The useful habit is to change one variable at a time. If you add thrust and your boat flips, you learned something. If you add thrust and armor simultaneously and it flips, you learned nothing.

## Build Advice That Holds Up

Because the game does not publish block names, stage counts, gold values, or shop prices in its official description, no honest guide can hand you a canonical blueprint. What transfers between players is process.

1. **Build small first.** A minimal hull that floats and moves is a better starting point than an ambitious ship that fails for three reasons at once.
2. **Test before you optimize.** Launch an early version, watch where it breaks, and fix that specific weakness.
3. **Keep critical components reachable.** If you must expose something, expose it deliberately rather than by accident.
4. **Iterate in place.** Since you are designing on a build plot, rebuilding is the normal workflow, not a punishment.
5. **Compare runs, not opinions.** Your own stage results are better evidence than any list of "best" builds.

For health-related decisions, remember the advertised group perks: Mega Member and Royal Member are listed at 300% more health, which changes how much risk a given hull can absorb. That is a documented perk, not a hidden mechanic.
