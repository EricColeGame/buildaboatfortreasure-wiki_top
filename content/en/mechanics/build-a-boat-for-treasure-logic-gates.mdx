## What the Game Actually Is

## Buoyancy, Weight, and Thrust: Design Reasoning Players Use

The following is player reasoning, not documented statistics. No official source publishes block names, weights, or thrust values, so everything here is general design logic that experienced builders tend to agree on.

Three forces decide whether your ship survives: buoyancy, weight, and thrust. Buoyant blocks keep you above the waterline, heavy blocks pull you down, and thrust blocks determine how much mass you can move and how quickly. A common mistake is treating the build plot like a sculpture gallery. A beautiful ship that floats too low will take on water at the first wave.

A few principles that hold up across most builds:

- Keep the center of mass low. Heavy components near the bottom resist tipping when obstacles shove you sideways.
- Spread thrust rather than concentrating it. One enormous engine pushes hard but is vulnerable; several smaller ones give you redundancy and better control.
- Protect the front. The bow absorbs the first impact of nearly every obstacle, so reinforcing it usually pays off more than reinforcing the stern.
- Test with a minimal design first. If a simple hull reaches a checkpoint, you know the foundation is sound before you add cargo, decoration, or extra engines.

Third-party guides frequently recommend iterating in short runs rather than building one massive ship and hoping. That advice is consistent with how the game rewards players with gold for progress, though the exact gold values per stage or obstacle are not documented officially and should not be treated as fixed.

## A Reasonable First Ship

If you are starting fresh, aim for a modest, wide hull with a low profile, a reinforced nose, and two or three thrust sources rather than one. Add a small amount of elevated structure only after the base survives. Then extend the design one obstacle at a time, keeping each change small enough that you can tell what caused a failure.

This will not guarantee success. Build A Boat For Treasure is deliberately punishing, and even strong designs meet obstacles that punish specific weaknesses. The goal is to fail in ways that teach you something.
