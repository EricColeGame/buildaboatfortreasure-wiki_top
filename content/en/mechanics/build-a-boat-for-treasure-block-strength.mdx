## The design triangle: buoyancy, weight, and thrust

Almost every decision on your build plot trades against three things. Treat this as player reasoning rather than documented mechanics:

- **Buoyancy:** your hull needs enough volume in contact with water to hold the whole craft up. Wider, sealed sections generally float better than tall, narrow towers.
- **Weight:** every block you add increases mass. Heavy armor low in the hull can help stability; heavy armor stacked high tends to make you top-heavy and tippy.
- **Thrust:** your propulsion has to overcome drag and your own mass. A light, streamlined boat with modest thrust often outruns a fortress with the same engine count.

The tension is the game. Add protection and you slow down; strip protection and you reach the next obstacle fragile. Most players iterate by adjusting one variable at a time, then sailing a few stages to see what changed.

## A repeatable build routine

A general workflow that keeps changes testable:

1. **Build a simple, sealed hull first.** Get something that floats before you get something that fights.
2. **Add propulsion and confirm you move.** A hull that floats but goes nowhere teaches you nothing about obstacle handling.
3. **Add structure incrementally.** Each addition should be justified by a problem you actually hit, not a problem you imagine.
4. **Keep the center of mass low.** In practice, players report that stable boats survive more stages than fast-but-flippy ones, though the exact thresholds are not documented anywhere.
5. **Test in short runs.** Rebuild before a run rather than after, so you always know which version failed.

Third-party guides often publish optimized designs and tier lists. Those are secondary observations from other players, not developer documentation, and they can go stale after updates. Use them as starting points, then verify in the game itself.
