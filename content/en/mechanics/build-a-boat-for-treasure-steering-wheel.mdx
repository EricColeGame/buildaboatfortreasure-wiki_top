## Start With Buoyancy and Weight, Not Speed

New builders usually add thrust first. That is backwards. A boat floats because the upward force from displaced water exceeds the downward force of its mass. Adding more engines does not change that balance, and it usually makes it worse by adding weight.

A practical approach is to build the smallest hull that can carry your intended payload. Wide, flat layouts tend to resist tipping. Tall, narrow towers tend to capsize, especially in stages with ramps or spinning obstacles. Keep heavy components low and near the center of the hull, because a high center of mass turns every bump into a roll.

Ask yourself a simple question at each step: does this part help me float, move, or survive? If it does none of those, it is ballast you did not choose.

## Thrust, Steering, and Control

Once a hull floats, propulsion becomes the interesting problem. Thrusters placed at the rear push you forward; thrusters placed at the sides or front can help you rotate. Symmetry matters more than raw count. Two matched engines on opposite sides behave predictably. Five mismatched engines produce a boat that curves left no matter what you do.

Community guides often suggest testing a design on an early, gentle stage before attempting anything harder. That is sound advice regardless of which blocks you use, because it isolates flaws. If a boat spins in calm water, it will spin worse in a hazard. Iterate on the plot, then commit to a run.

## Protection and Damage Tolerance

Obstacles in Build A Boat For Treasure punish exposed structure. The reasoning here is the same as in any physics game: a part that is armored costs weight, and a part that is unprotected costs the whole run when it breaks.

Layer your critical components. Put engines and the core of the hull behind or inside structural material rather than on the outside edge. If you use explosives, remember that the Royal Member perk grants additional TNT barrels, which changes how much risk you can absorb but does not make TNT safe to carry casually.

There is no documented "best" armor block, and any list claiming exact durability numbers should be treated as a third-party observation. The reliable test is your own: build, launch, and watch which section fails first.

## A Repeatable Build-and-Test Loop

A useful routine for any builder looks like this:

1. Sketch the smallest hull that floats with your payload.
2. Add matched thrust and confirm the boat tracks straight on calm water.
3. Add protection around engines and the hull core, then re-check buoyancy.
4. Run an easy stage and note the first failure point.
5. Rebuild that section only, rather than starting over.

This loop respects the fact that gold is earned per successful progress, and the advertised group perks increase that gold by 25% for members and Mega Members. If you are farming, membership is a documented multiplier. Anything beyond that is speculation.
