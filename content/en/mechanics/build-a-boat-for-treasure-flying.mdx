## Core Design Reasoning: Buoyancy, Weight, and Thrust

This section is player reasoning about design principles, not documented stats. Still, the logic is grounded in how physics-based boat builders generally behave, and it is the most useful mental model for newcomers.

Every block you place adds weight. Buoyancy comes from how much of your structure displaces water, which is why wide, flat bottoms tend to float more reliably than tall, narrow towers. Thrust has to overcome drag from the water and from your own mass. That gives you three competing pressures:

- **Buoyancy:** more submerged volume helps you float, but water can also flood open designs.
- **Weight:** heavy armor protects you but sinks you and slows acceleration.
- **Thrust:** more propulsion helps you climb over obstacles, but engines add mass and can be knocked off.

A practical approach is to build a stable, low base first, test it on early obstacles, and only then add protection or speed. If your boat flips easily, the problem is usually center of mass, not raw power. If it stalls on climbs, the problem is usually thrust-to-weight ratio. If it breaks apart, the problem is usually connection points and exposed critical parts.

## How to Get Better Without Guessing

The most reliable improvement loop in a physics builder is disciplined iteration:

1. Change one variable per test run — width, engine placement, or armor, not all three.
2. Note what failed: flooding, flipping, stalling, or disintegrating.
3. Rebuild the specific weak section rather than starting over.
4. Keep a design that survives early stages before optimizing for speed.

Testing on the earliest obstacles is free practice. There is no documented shortcut that replaces that iteration, and no guide can promise your design will survive a full run.
