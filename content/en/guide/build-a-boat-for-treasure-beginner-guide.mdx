The premise is in the name. You get a build plot, you assemble a vessel out of blocks, and you send it into a course full of hazards. Gold is the currency you accumulate, and the official page ties gold bonuses to group membership.

What the official page does **not** document: the number of stages, specific block names, shop prices, blueprint lists, promo codes, or hotkeys. Third-party guides discuss these things, and you can read them as secondary observations from other players, but they are not developer documentation. If a number matters to you, verify it in the game itself.

The safest mental model is that the game rewards iteration. You build something, you learn what breaks, and you build again.

## Design reasoning that holds up

The following is player reasoning, not documented mechanics. It is the kind of thinking that shows up repeatedly in community discussion because it follows from simple physics-style tradeoffs.

**Buoyancy and weight.** Blocks add mass. More mass generally means the craft sits lower and is harder to push. Keeping the hull lean usually beats armoring every surface, at least early on.

**Thrust and control.** A boat that moves fast but cannot be steered is not much use in a hazard course. Balanced propulsion tends to survive longer than maximum speed.

**Protection where it counts.** If you know a hazard comes from a particular direction, reinforcing that side is more efficient than wrapping the whole build. This is a design principle, not a claim about any specific stage.

**Redundancy.** If one structural section fails and the rest still floats and moves, you may still finish the run. Building in separated sections is a common approach players describe.

**Iterate cheaply.** Early builds should be disposable. You learn more from five quick attempts than from one over-engineered attempt that you are afraid to risk.

## Group membership: worth checking, not worth assuming

The advertised perks give members a gold multiplier and higher tiers additional health and TNT. If you play regularly, a gold bonus compounds over time, so it is reasonable to at least compare the group's requirements against how much you actually play.

What you should not do is assume the perks work exactly as you imagine. The official page lists percentages; it does not explain edge cases. Test it, watch your gold before and after, and decide from your own observations.
