## How Building Actually Works, as Player Reasoning

The official listing describes a game about building a ship from blocks on a build plot and sailing it through obstacle-filled stages to earn gold. It does not publish block names, stage counts, gold values, or a blueprint library. Anything claiming to list those is third-party material at best.

What follows is general design reasoning that applies to any physics-based boat builder. It is not documented game data.

**Buoyancy versus weight.** Blocks that keep you afloat and blocks that protect you compete for the same space. A hull that is too heavy sinks before the first obstacle. A hull that is too light tips or flips when something hits it broadside. Wide, low shapes generally resist tipping better than tall, narrow ones, because the center of mass sits closer to the water.

**Thrust and control.** A single engine on the centerline pushes you forward but gives you little ability to correct course. Spreading propulsion so you can steer reduces the number of times you ram an obstacle at full speed, which is where most runs end.

**Protection where it counts.** Armor on the nose is worth more than armor everywhere, because the front of the boat meets obstacles first. Shielding the rear is usually wasted mass unless the stage design punishes you from behind, which is something you learn only by playing.

**Rebuild, do not perfect.** Because the loop is build, sail, fail, rebuild, the fastest progress usually comes from small, targeted fixes after each attempt rather than a full redesign.

Third-party guides make many of the same points in more detail. Attribute their specifics to them, not to the developer, and expect them to drift out of date as the game is updated.
