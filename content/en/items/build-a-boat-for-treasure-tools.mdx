The structure of the game is straightforward. You get a build plot, you place blocks to form a vessel, and you send it into a course of obstacles. Reaching the end means treasure, and treasure generally means gold you can put back into better builds.

## How to think about building a ship

Everything in this section is **player reasoning**, not documented game data. The developers have not published a design manual, so treat these as general principles that experienced players tend to agree on.

**Buoyancy and weight.** A vessel that floats is one whose weight is distributed well enough that it does not tip or sink. Piling on heavy material in one spot tends to cause problems, while spreading mass across the base usually helps stability.

**Thrust and control.** Propulsion gets you moving, but control keeps you from becoming a permanent fixture on the first obstacle. Builds that turn and stop predictably are often more useful than builds that only go fast.

**Protection.** Obstacles hit hard. Reinforcing the front and the areas around anything critical is a common approach, though the tradeoff is always added weight.

**Iteration.** Expect to fail, then rebuild. The cheapest way to learn what works is to launch, watch what breaks, and adjust one thing at a time. Community guides frequently make this point, and it holds up regardless of which blocks you have unlocked.

If you want a starting philosophy: build something small, stable, and cheap first. Confirm it can survive a little punishment before you invest in a larger design.
