## Materials and Blocks: Choosing the Right Building Pieces

Tools only get you so far — the blocks you place with them decide how your boat handles. Broadly speaking, parts fall into a few families based on weight and durability.

| Block Family | Weight | Durability | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light wood and plank parts | Low | Moderate | Beginner hulls, decks, basic frames |
| Plastic and decorative blocks | Very low | Low | Topside detail, signage, trim |
| Reinforced or heavy parts | High | High | Hull armor, impact zones, keels |
| Glass and transparent parts | Low | Low | Windows, cockpits, style points |
| Lift-generating parts | Varies | Fragile | Airborne builds and gliding shortcuts |

The trade-off is always the same: heavier parts survive collisions better but drag your boat down and demand more propulsion. Lighter parts are fast and cheap but shatter on impact. Most successful builds mix both — a heavy keel and reinforced bow, with light materials everywhere else.

If you are unsure where to start, build a wide, flat hull out of light blocks, then reinforce only the front and the underside. That gives you a stable platform without turning your boat into an anchor.

### How Weight Affects Your Build

Weight is not just about speed. It also affects how quickly your boat recovers after a bad landing or a collision with terrain. A heavy boat sinks fast when it takes on water; a light boat flips easily in rough sections. Player experience suggests testing a new design on a short, easy run before committing to a long treasure voyage.

## Propulsion Tools: Turning a Floating Box Into a Boat

A hull with no propulsion is just a raft. The propulsion parts you attach — and how you attach them — decide whether you reach the treasure or drift in circles.

| Propulsion Type | How It Works | Strength | Weakness | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sails | Catch the wind | Cheap and simple | Depends on wind direction | Early stages and short runs |
| Thrusters | Push with constant force | Reliable speed and control | Heavier builds, more parts | Long voyages and precision steering |
| Wheels | Move along surfaces | Great on ramps and land | Useless in open water | Hybrid boat-and-lander designs |
| Balloons or lift parts | Provide upward force | Reaches high routes | Fragile and hard to control | Airborne shortcuts and gliding |

Symmetry matters here. If you place a single thruster off-center, your boat will pull to one side for the entire trip. Place propulsion in matched pairs, then use the Paint Tool to mark the left and right units so you can spot a mistake at a glance.

Many players also keep a small backup propulsion source — even a single sail — in case their main engine gets knocked off by an obstacle. It is a cheap insurance policy that has saved plenty of runs.

## Common Tool Mistakes and Player Experience Tips

Even experienced builders repeat a few of these mistakes. Here are the ones that come up most often in community discussions.

| Mistake | What Happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping welds | Boat flexes and breaks apart | Weld hull seams before launching |
| Overbuilding | Part count climbs, performance drops | Remove decorative parts you do not need |
| Single off-center thruster | Boat veers constantly | Use matched pairs, left and right |
| Top-heavy design | Boat tips on turns and landings | Keep heavy parts low in the hull |
| Ignoring weight | Slow, sluggish movement | Mix light materials with a few heavy ones |
| No test run | Failure deep into a voyage | Always test on a short route first |

A few tips that come up again and again from player experience:

- **Build symmetrical whenever possible.** Copy one side of your design to the other instead of eyeballing it.
- **Keep a template boat.** Once you have a design that works, save it and modify it for new stages rather than starting from scratch.
- **Color-code everything.** A two-second paint job can save you five minutes of hunting for a broken part.
- **Watch your part count.** Very large builds can strain performance, especially on lower-end devices.
- **Reinforce the front.** The bow takes the most hits, so give it the heaviest, toughest parts you have.
- **Practice in a private or low-stakes run.** Learning how a new propulsion type handles is much cheaper when there is no treasure on the line.

Above all, remember that the build a boat for treasure tools are means to an end. A simple, well-welded, symmetrical boat will outperform a chaotic pile of rare blocks almost every time.
