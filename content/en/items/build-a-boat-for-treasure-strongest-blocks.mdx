## Design Reasoning for a Ship That Survives

The sources here do not document specific block names, stage counts, gold values, shop prices, or blueprint lists, so this section stays at the level of general design reasoning — the kind of thinking players apply, not documented statistics.

Think about your boat as four competing budgets:

- **Buoyancy.** A vessel needs enough low, wide support to stay upright. Spreading mass outward and keeping the center of gravity low resists tipping when obstacles shove you sideways.
- **Weight.** Every block you add is something your propulsion has to push. Heavier builds tend to be more stable but slower and harder to steer. There is a real trade-off here, and the right balance depends on the stage you are attempting.
- **Thrust.** Forward force needs to overcome drag and your own mass. Adding more engines can help, but only if your structure can take the stress without tearing apart.
- **Protection.** Some stages punish exposed parts of your build. Armoring critical sections — engines, the core of the hull — can keep you moving after a hit, though armor adds weight.

A practical approach many players describe: start with a minimal, cheap design that floats and moves, test it on early stages, then add one feature at a time. Change one variable per run so you can tell what actually helped. Because the official page documents stages and obstacles but not their specifics, the only reliable way to learn a stage is to attempt it and observe.
