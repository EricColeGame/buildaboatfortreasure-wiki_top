## How a Single Chest Run Plays Out

Every attempt follows the same five beats. Knowing where runs usually fail is half the battle.

| Phase | What you do | What usually goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Build phase | Place blocks in the build zone, balancing weight against buoyancy | Overbuilding — a heavy boat sinks before the first turn |
| Launch | Drop into the river and take the controls | Steering into the first hazard while still accelerating |
| River run | Dodge saws, logs, cannons, spikes, and gaps | Losing the front of the hull early and dragging the rest |
| Final approach | Line up with the chest and hold your course | Capsizing a few blocks from the payout |
| Payout | Touch the chest, collect gold, advance | Leaving before the reward registers |

The pattern is consistent: most players lose runs in the build phase, not the river. A boat that is too heavy, too wide, or too fragile never gets a chance to prove itself, no matter how good the driving is.

One more thing worth internalizing: the payout only counts when your boat — or at least a surviving piece of it — reaches the chest. If your creation breaks apart halfway down the course, you keep nothing. That single rule shapes every smart build decision in the game.

### A Simple, Repeatable Farming Recipe

If you are building your first dedicated chest-farming boat, keep it boring:

- Start with a narrow hull — three to five blocks wide is usually enough.
- Keep the center of mass low by putting heavy blocks on the bottom layer.
- Add two or three balloons as insurance against a bad drop or a gap.
- Armor only the front and the underside, where hits actually land.
- Leave the back light so the boat self-corrects instead of nose-diving.

Then run the same stage over and over, adjusting one thing at a time. Changing five variables at once teaches you nothing.

## Practical Tips to Reach the Chest More Often

Small habits separate players who finish runs from players who watch their boat sink.

| Common mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Armoring everything | Weight destroys buoyancy | Armor only the front and underside |
| Skipping balloons | No recovery from a bad drop | Add a few as cheap insurance |
| Building too wide | Wide hulls snag on obstacles | Keep the profile narrow |
| Practicing only the opening | The final stretch is the hardest part | Replay stages you have already beaten |
| Rebuilding from scratch every run | Wasted time between attempts | Memorize one design and reuse it |

A few more tips that come up constantly in player experience:

- **Test before you commit.** Do a short shakedown run on an easy stage before taking a new design into harder water.
- **Watch the last twenty percent.** That is where the chest sits and where most boats fall apart.
- **Replace cheap blocks often.** A single missing plank can change how your boat handles.
- **Play with a friend.** Shared runs let one person drive while the other watches for side chests.
- **Do not chase every side path.** Bonus chests are only worth it when your boat is stable enough to get back on route.
