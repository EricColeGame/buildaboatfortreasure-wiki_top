## Core Design Reasoning: Buoyancy, Weight, Thrust, Protection

The official page does not publish building advice, so the following is player reasoning drawn from how physics-based boat builders generally behave. Treat it as a mental model, not a stat sheet.

Four forces fight over your build:

- **Buoyancy** keeps you above the waterline.
- **Weight** pulls you down and changes how you sit in the water.
- **Thrust** moves you forward and determines whether you can outrun or outlast a hazard.
- **Protection** decides what survives contact with an obstacle.

The classic beginner mistake is overbuilding. More blocks mean more mass, and mass without matching buoyancy means a boat that sits low, turns sluggishly, and floods the moment something knocks a hole in the hull. A lighter, wider craft with a clear purpose usually outperforms a sprawling fortress.

A second common mistake is treating thrust as optional. Boats that cannot accelerate cannot recover from being pushed backward or sideways, and many obstacles reward momentum rather than raw durability.

Finally, think about where damage lands. If your most important structure — whatever keeps the boat afloat and moving — sits at the very front, it absorbs every impact first. Players who protect the core and sacrifice outer layers generally report better runs, though again, this is community reasoning rather than documented design.

## Practical Tips for Newer Players

- **Start small.** A compact, stable craft teaches you the physics faster than a huge one.
- **Test before committing.** Sail a short distance and watch how your boat sits in the water before adding more structure.
- **Rebuild rather than patch.** Iteration is the actual gameplay loop here.
