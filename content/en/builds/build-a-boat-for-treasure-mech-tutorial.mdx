## Buoyancy, Weight, and Thrust: Reasoning It Out

This next part is player reasoning, not documented statistics. The game does not publish a physics table, so treat these as mental models rather than rules.

- **Keep the center of mass low.** A heavy hull sitting beneath light deck blocks resists tipping. Stacking mass high makes every wave and ramp a chance to capsize.
- **Spread weight evenly across the hull.** A lopsided build tends to veer or roll, and correcting it mid-stage is much harder than fixing it on the plot.
- **Reserve buoyancy before adding cargo.** If your design already sits low in the water at launch, adding more blocks rarely improves things.
- **Think about thrust direction, not just thrust amount.** Propulsion that pushes you off-axis wastes energy fighting your own hull.
- **Protect the parts you cannot replace.** Enclosing critical components behind cheaper outer blocks is a common community habit, though which blocks count as "critical" depends entirely on your own design.

The practical takeaway is to test in small increments. Change one variable, sail, observe, and adjust. Because the game's official page documents no specific block stats, any precise numbers you see online should be treated with suspicion.

## Building for Obstacles Rather Than Open Water

Stages in Build a Boat for Treasure are obstacle courses, not calm seas. That changes what "good design" means. A stable but slow ship may survive hazards a fast, fragile one cannot, and vice versa, depending on what the stage actually throws at you.

A useful habit is to separate your build into two questions. First, does it float level with the load you intend to carry? Second, can it survive contact? Armor, bracing, and redundancy all trade speed for durability, and there is no single correct ratio. Third-party guides often describe stage-specific tactics, and those can be genuinely helpful, but they reflect one player's observations rather than developer documentation. Cross-check anything that sounds like an exact figure.

## Common Mistakes New Builders Make

Some recurring problems show up regardless of skill level, and they are worth naming plainly.

- **Overbuilding before testing.** A huge ship that has never been sailed tells you nothing.
- **Ignoring the build plot's limits.** Whatever you design has to leave the plot intact.
- **Chasing codes that are not documented.** If you cannot find an entry field in the game, there is nothing to redeem.
- **Assuming one build fits every stage.** Adaptation usually beats a single universal design.
- **Trusting exact numbers from unofficial sources.** The developer page documents perks and identity, not stats.
