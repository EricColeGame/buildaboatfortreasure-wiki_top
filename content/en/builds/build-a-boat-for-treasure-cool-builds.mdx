## The build plot: what you are actually doing

The build plot is where every run begins. You place blocks, then launch the boat into the course. Everything past that point is a test of whether your design survives contact with the obstacles.

Because the official page does not publish block names, material stats, weights, or prices, this article will not invent them. What follows is general design reasoning that players tend to arrive at after a few failed runs — it is not developer documentation and should not be read as a stat sheet.

Four ideas drive most build decisions:

**Buoyancy and weight distribution.** A boat floats when it displaces enough water for its mass. Spreading heavy blocks wide and low keeps the center of gravity down, which makes the craft harder to flip when something clips it from the side.

**Thrust and steering.** A run only ends when you reach the treasure, so forward progress matters. Players generally want enough propulsion to push through rough sections and enough steering authority to sidestep hazards rather than absorb them.

**Protection.** Some obstacles are simply too wide to dodge. A sacrificial layer of cheap blocks at the front can eat a hit that would otherwise reach the core of the boat.

**Redundancy.** If a single block is load-bearing and it breaks, the run ends. Duplicating structural paths costs more gold but keeps a damaged boat moving.

None of that is a guarantee of success. It is a starting framework for your own experimentation, and the fastest way to learn is to run short, cheap designs and watch where they fail.

## Practical approach for your first serious build

Start small and cheap. Build a hull wide enough to be stable, keep the heaviest blocks low, and add just enough propulsion to move forward with control. Run it, and note exactly what ends the run: a flip, a break, or simply not enough speed.

Then fix one variable at a time. If you flip, widen the base before you add power. If you break, add a sacrificial front section before you add weight. If you stall, add thrust before you add armor. Fixing two things at once makes it impossible to know which change mattered.

Once a design survives a few stages, that is the moment to consider whether a group membership bonus is worth it for your goals. The advertised gold bonus compounds over many runs, and the advertised health bonus changes what your boat can survive — but whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on how often you play.
