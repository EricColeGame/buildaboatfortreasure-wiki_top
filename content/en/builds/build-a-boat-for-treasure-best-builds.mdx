## Building a boat: the reasoning that actually holds up

The official description does not document block names, part stats, prices, or a blueprint list, and we will not invent them. What follows is player reasoning about design trade-offs, not developer documentation.

Almost every boat decision comes down to four competing pressures:

- **Buoyancy**: you need enough low-density or floating material to keep the hull above the waterline once cargo and passengers are aboard.
- **Weight**: every block you add costs you somewhere. More mass means slower acceleration and a deeper draft.
- **Thrust**: propulsion determines whether you can outrun an obstacle or whether you simply arrive at it sooner.
- **Protection**: armor and shielding help you survive impacts, but they add mass, which fights your buoyancy and thrust.

The practical consequence is that a boat which is excellent at one stage is often bad at the next. A wide, stable platform survives early hazards comfortably but may struggle when a course demands speed. A narrow, fast hull may clear distance quickly and then disintegrate the moment something heavy clips it. Experienced builders tend to iterate rather than commit: change one variable, test it, and note what actually failed.

A simple rule of thumb many players apply is to build the smallest vessel that still completes the job. Extra blocks are extra failure points. Reinforcing a single critical joint is usually cheaper than armoring the entire ship.

## Common beginner mistakes

Most early failures on the build plot trace back to the same handful of causes.

**Overbuilding before testing.** New players often construct an elaborate multi-deck ship before running a single stage. Because nothing is documented about optimal layouts, the only real feedback loop is launching and watching what breaks. Build small, launch early, then expand.

**Treating the first successful run as a finished design.** A stage that was easy in one attempt can be brutal in another if your approach speed or angle shifts. Robustness matters more than a single clean run.

**Ignoring the server size.** The listed server size of seven means the experience is fairly intimate. Fewer players per server can mean less lag and more consistent physics, which is genuinely useful when your boat's survival depends on precise movement.

**Assuming third-party guides are current.** Community guides are secondary observations written by players, and they may describe mechanics from older versions. Cross-check anything specific against what you can see in the game today.
