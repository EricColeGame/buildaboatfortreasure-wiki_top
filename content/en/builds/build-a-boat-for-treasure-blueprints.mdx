## Why your boat floats or flops

Buoyancy in this game follows the same intuition as real physics, even if the exact numbers are not published. A wide, flat footprint spreads weight over more water and resists tipping. A tall, narrow tower of blocks does the opposite. When players say their boat "randomly" flipped, the cause is usually a center of mass sitting too high above the waterline.

Here is the reasoning players apply, not a documented formula:

- Keep heavy structural blocks low and close to the waterline.
- Build outward before you build upward.
- Balance the load left and right, not just front to back.
- Treat every decorative block as added weight with a cost.

You can test this yourself in seconds. Build a simple flat platform, sail it, then rebuild it twice as tall with the same footprint. The difference in stability will teach you more than any guide.

## Thrust, steering, and the draft problem

A boat that floats perfectly but cannot move is still a failure. Propulsion in Build A Boat For Treasure comes from blocks you place, and the trade-offs are consistent. More thrust usually means more weight, which lowers your boat in the water and increases drag. A hull sitting too deep becomes sluggish and easier to swamp.

Practical reasoning that holds up across most builds:

1. Add propulsion in pairs so your boat tracks straight instead of spinning.
2. Place steering control near the rear, where it has leverage.
3. Test thrust on a calm launch before adding armor, so you can judge the base handling.
4. If your boat veers, adjust symmetry first before adding more power.

None of this depends on undocumented stats. It is cause and effect you can observe on your own plot.

## Surviving the obstacle course

The stages between your launch and the treasure are built around hazards, and the official description's mention of TNT barrels for Royal Members tells you explosions are part of the design. Protection and speed pull against each other. Armor blocks absorb punishment but add mass, and that mass costs you acceleration and buoyancy.

A reasonable approach for newer players is staged testing. Build a light, fast frame and see how far it gets. Then add protection to the specific area that failed. Players who bolt on heavy shielding from the start often cannot tell which change actually helped. Since the stage layout and hazard behavior are not documented on the official page, treat any third-party stage list as a community observation and verify it in game.
