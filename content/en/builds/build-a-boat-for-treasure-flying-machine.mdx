If you have spent any time on Roblox, you have probably seen a thumbnail of a wooden raft exploding against a wall of spikes. That is **Build a Boat for Treasure**, a Chillz Studios experience that has quietly outlasted most of the platform's trends. It was created on November 2, 2016, and it was still being updated as recently as October 9, 2026, according to its official listing. At the time of that record, the place page showed roughly 5.2 billion visits and about 8,000 players online, with a listed server size of 7 and an Adventure genre tag.

You can find the official page here: [Build A Boat For Treasure on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/537413528/Build-A-Boat-For-Treasure).

If you are new, the appeal is simple: you get a build plot, you assemble a vessel out of blocks, and then you sail it into a course full of hazards to collect gold. If you are returning after a long break, the core loop is unchanged, but it helps to know exactly what the developers advertise versus what players merely believe.

## What the Official Page Actually Advertises

The official description is short, and it is worth reading literally rather than through the lens of a guide site. It advertises group perks for members of Chillz Studios:

That is the entire documented perk table. Notably, the page does **not** document a promo-code system, a shop price list, a block catalog, or a stage count. Third-party guides often fill those gaps with numbers, but those are community observations, not developer documentation. If you see a claim about a specific gold payout per stage or a price for a particular block, treat it as unverified until you see it in your own session.

## Building a Boat: What You Are Actually Solving

The verified facts do not describe individual blocks, so any block advice below is general design reasoning, not documented stats. With that caveat, the build phase is essentially a physics problem with three competing demands.

**Buoyancy versus weight.** Every block you add is mass. More mass means the vessel sits lower and moves sluggishly. Players generally reason that you want the hull compact and the deck light, because a top-heavy craft tips on the first turn. This is player reasoning, not a documented formula.

**Thrust versus control.** A single large engine can push a heavy boat, but it can also make the boat uncontrollable in tight sections. Spreading propulsion or adding steering surfaces is a common community approach, though nothing on the official page specifies how thrust is calculated.

**Protection versus cost.** Armor plating helps against spikes, cannon fire, and other hazards, but it adds weight and, in some cases, expense. The recurring tradeoff is deciding which hazards are worth armoring against and which are better avoided through speed or routing.

A practical habit: build small, launch, watch where you fail, then add exactly one thing to fix that failure. Iterating in small steps teaches you more than copying a large blueprint you do not understand.

## Running the Stages and Earning Gold

Gold is the resource the official description clearly cares about, since every advertised group perk is expressed in gold or health terms. That tells you the intended loop: sail, survive, earn, upgrade. What the official page does not tell you is how much gold any particular stage yields, whether failure costs you anything, or how many stages exist in total.

For those specifics, the only reliable method is direct observation. Run a stage, note your gold before and after, and record it yourself. Community-maintained spreadsheets exist, but they are third-party observations and can lag behind updates.

The advertised health bonuses are worth understanding, too. Mega Member and Royal Member both advertise 300% more health. If you are hitting a wall repeatedly, the group membership perks may matter more to your progress than a redesign of your boat. Royal Member additionally advertises +4 barrels of TNT, which suggests a more offensive playstyle is viable, though the official page does not explain how that TNT is deployed.

## Group Membership: Is It Worth It?

The membership question is the one area where the official page gives you something concrete to compare. A Member gets 25% more gold. That is straightforward and stacks with your normal earnings, since the perk is phrased as an increase rather than a replacement.

Mega Member trades that same 25% gold bonus for 300% more health. If your boats tend to break rather than run out of time, the health multiplier is the more relevant perk. Royal Member combines both the health bonus and the gold bonus and adds TNT.

What the page does **not** state is the cost of any tier, whether the perks apply automatically, or whether they expire. Those details live in the group page, not the game description, and you should check the group directly before joining rather than trusting a secondary source.

## Common Misconceptions

**"There are promo codes."** There is no redemption system documented on the official game page. If codes exist, they are not documented by the developer through the primary listing. To verify, check the official page and the Chillz Studios group announcements rather than a code aggregator.

**"It's the same as the Build a Boat obby app."** It is not. There is an unrelated mobile app with a similar name that is a different product entirely. The Roblox experience by Chillz Studios is the one with the visit count and the group perks described above.

**"Someone published the full stage list."** Nobody has published an official one, because the developer has not documented stage counts. Community lists are observations and may be outdated after any update.
